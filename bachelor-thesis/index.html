Abstract

Belyi's theorem states that a compact Riemann surface can be defined over the field of algebraic numbers if and only if it admits a holomorphic map to the Riemann sphere that is ramified over at most three points. Grothendieck observed that such Belyi maps correspond to dessins d'enfants: bipartite graphs embedded in a surface. This thesis presents a proof of Belyi's theorem and develops the correspondence between Belyi pairs, dessins d'enfants and finite-index subgroups of the fundamental group of the thrice-punctured sphere, as well as the resulting action of the absolute Galois group Gal(ℚ/ℚ) on dessins.
